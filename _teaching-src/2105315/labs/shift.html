Check the AI's code

The consultant asked an AI assistant: "Test whether benzene changed after the date." It returned this.

df = pd.read_csv("benzene_hourly.csv", parse_dates=["datetime"])before = df.loc[df.period == "before", "C6H6_ugm3"].dropna()after = df.loc[df.period == "after", "C6H6_ugm3"].dropna()t, p = stats.ttest_ind(after, before, equal_var=False)print(f"n = {len(before) + len(after)}, p = {p:.1e}")# "p < 0.05: the traffic plan changed benzene."

The code runs and the p is computed correctly for what it was given. Which assumption of the t-test does this data break, and what does that do to p?

Compare with the explanation

The t-test treats every value as an independent measurement. Hourly benzene is not: each hour is close to the hour before (the correlation between neighboring hours is about 0.8 at this station), and every day repeats the same rush-hour pattern. About 650 hours carry far less information than 650 independent values, so the standard error is too small and p is far too small.

The comment adds a second error: even a real difference says nothing about what caused it. Weather, holidays and the season change from one fortnight to the next as well.

daily = df.set_index("datetime").resample("D")["C6H6_ugm3"].agg(["mean", "count"]) daily = daily[daily["count"] >= 18] # then test the daily means, and compare with ordinary dates (Step 7)

Test the daily means

One value per day: 14 before against 14 after. Both tests are Welch's t-test, which does not assume that the two periods have the same spread. The 95 % confidence interval is the range of differences in the means that fits the data.

Placebo dates

Run the same comparison at every date in the record. Nothing was changed on purpose at those dates, so a fair test should call about 5 % of them significant.

08

Check your understanding

Write first, then compare.

Even the test on daily means calls about a quarter of the ordinary dates significant. Why is it still too eager?

Compare with the explanation

Daily means are also not independent: one day is like the next (the correlation between neighboring days is about 0.6 here), because weather patterns last several days. And the level moves with the season and with holidays, so two fortnights differ even when nothing was changed. The t-test only knows the scatter within each fortnight; it does not know how much fortnights differ from each other in an ordinary year. The placebo dates measure exactly that.

Write the sentence you would send to the city about your date: what changed, by how much, and how it compares with ordinary dates.

Compare with the explanation

A defensible sentence gives the size of the difference with its interval, and the placebo comparison, and does not name a cause the data cannot separate. For Demo A: "Mean daily benzene was 1.7 µg/m³ lower in the 14 days after 29 July 2004 than before (95 % confidence interval −3.9 to 0.5). A difference this large or larger occurred at 45 % of the dates in the year with no planned change, so these data do not show an effect of the new traffic plan." For Demo B the difference, −5.9 µg/m³, was larger than at all but one ordinary date: a real shift, but the data alone cannot say whether the traffic plan or the November weather caused it.
