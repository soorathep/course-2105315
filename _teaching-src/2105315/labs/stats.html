The statistics you need for Weeks 5 and 6

Weeks 5 and 6 use standard deviation, correlation, the t-test, p and confidence intervals. This page explains each one with numbers from the labs, and the one line of pandas or scipy that computes it. Work through it with a calculator; the self-check at the end takes ten minutes.

01

Mean and standard deviation σ

How large the values are, and how much they scatter.

The mean is the sum divided by the number of values, n. The standard deviation σ is the typical distance from the mean:

σ = √( Σ (x − mean)² / (n − 1) )

By hand. Five daily mean benzene values from Lab 06, Demo A: 12.55, 12.40, 8.62, 6.05, 12.13 µg/m³. Mean = 51.75 / 5 = 10.35. Distances from the mean: 2.20, 2.05, −1.73, −4.30, 1.78. Squared and added: 33.69. Divided by n − 1 = 4: 8.42. Square root: σ = 2.90 µg/m³.

s.mean(), s.std()   # pandas divides by n − 1, as above

numpy's np.std(x) divides by n, not n − 1, and gives a slightly smaller value (2.60 here). If your number differs from the lab's by a few percent, check which one your code used.

02

z-score and the 3σ rule

How unusual one value is.

z = (x − mean) / σ

z = 2 means two standard deviations above the mean. For data that follow a normal distribution, about 0.3 % of values lie more than 3σ from the mean, so "|z| > 3" is a common flag (Lab 03, Lab 05). It assumes one mean and one σ describe all the data; a daily cycle breaks that (Session 5).

03

Correlation r

Whether two variables move together along a straight line.

r runs from −1 (one rises as the other falls, exactly on a line) through 0 (no straight-line relation) to +1. r² is the share of the variation of one that a straight line through the other accounts for, whatever the cause.

df["T_degC"].corr(df["NOx_ppb"])

Lab 05, Demo A: r = −0.57 over 16 summer weeks, and 0.14 after removing the seasonal trend from both. r measures moving together; it does not say which one drives the other, or whether a third variable drives both.

04

How sure is a mean? The standard error

A mean of a few values is less certain than a mean of many.

standard error of a mean = σ / √n

The 14 daily means before the change in Lab 06, Demo A have mean 10.27 and σ = 3.45, so the standard error is 3.45 / √14 = 0.92 µg/m³. With 56 days it would be half that.

This holds only if the n values are independent: each tells you something the others do not. Neighboring hours of benzene are not (Session 12). Using 336 hours as n makes the standard error far too small.

05

Comparing two means: Welch's t-test

Is the difference larger than the scatter would produce by chance?

t = (meanafter − meanbefore) / √( σa²/na + σb²/nb )

Lab 06, Demo A, daily means: before 10.27 (σ 3.45, n 14), after 8.58 (σ 2.05, n 14). Difference −1.69. The bottom line: √(3.45²/14 + 2.05²/14) = 1.07. So t = −1.69 / 1.07 = −1.57.

Welch's version does not assume the two periods have the same σ; that is why the labs use it (equal_var=False).

from scipy import statsres = stats.ttest_ind(after, before, equal_var=False)print(res.statistic, res.pvalue, res.confidence_interval())

For this example scipy prints t = −1.57, p = 0.13, and an interval from −3.91 to 0.54.

06

What p and the 95 % confidence interval say

And what they do not say.

p

If nothing had changed, the share of comparisons that would show a difference at least this large. p = 0.13: about one in eight. It is not the probability that the change worked, and not the size of the change. 0.05 is a convention, not a law.

95 % confidence interval

The range of differences that fit the data: difference ± about 2 standard errors. Here −1.69 ± 2.08 × 1.07 = −3.91 to 0.54. It includes 0, so "no change" fits too. Its width shows how much the data can tell.

Both rest on the same assumptions as the standard error: independent values, and nothing else changing between the two periods. Session 12 tests both on plant data.

07

Self-check

Write your answer first, then open the explanation. Nothing is saved or sent.

1. A pandas column of daily means gives s.std() = 2.90 and np.std(s) = 2.60. Which does the lab use, and why do they differ?

Compare with the answer

The lab uses 2.90, pandas' default, which divides by n − 1. numpy divides by n. With few values the difference is noticeable; with hundreds it is tiny.

2. Daily temperature and daily NOx have r = −0.6. Write one sentence the data allow, and one they do not.

Compare with the answer

Allowed: "On colder days NOx tended to be higher over these weeks (r = −0.6)." Not allowed: "Cold weather causes high NOx" or "temperature explains 36 % of NOx", because r cannot separate temperature from anything else that changes with the season.

3. Two periods of 14 daily means each have σ = 2. Roughly what is the standard error of the difference in means?

Compare with the answer

√(2²/14 + 2²/14) = √(0.571) ≈ 0.76. A difference of about 1.5 (2 standard errors) would just reach p ≈ 0.05.

4. A t-test on 650 hourly values gives p = 0.00006; the same data as 28 daily means give p = 0.13. Which do you report, and why?

Compare with the answer

The daily means. Neighboring hours are not independent, so counting 650 of them makes the standard error far too small and p far too small. 28 daily means are closer to independent, though not fully (Session 12).

5. A 95 % confidence interval for a change in yield is +0.1 to +0.3 percentage points, p = 0.001. Is the change important?

Compare with the answer

It is very likely real (the interval excludes 0), but whether it matters depends on its size: 0.1 to 0.3 points may or may not pay for the change. Significance and importance are different questions.
