04

Check the AI's code

The report's author asked an AI assistant: "Make a bar chart that shows the difference between the two months clearly." It returned this.

monthly = df["C6H6_ugm3"].resample("MS").mean()fig, ax = plt.subplots()ax.bar(monthly.index.strftime("%b %Y"), monthly.values)ax.set_ylim(int(0.9 * monthly.min()), monthly.max() * 1.05)   # zoom in on the differenceax.set_title("Benzene was far worse in the second month")

The numbers on the bars are correct. What makes the figure mislead, and how would you notice?

Compare with the explanation

A bar shows a value by its length. The set_ylim line starts the axis just below the smaller mean, so most of each bar is cut off and what remains is mostly the difference. A 30 % difference can look like a bar three or four times taller. The data are right; the drawing is not.

You notice by reading the axis: if a bar chart's axis does not start at zero, compare the numbers, not the heights. The title then states a conclusion the figure has manufactured, and a monthly mean hides the spread of about 700 hours, most of which overlap between the two months.

ax.set_ylim(0, None) # bars start at zero # or: points with the interquartile range, and a title that states the numbers

08

Check your understanding

Write first, then compare.

A bar chart must start at zero. A line chart of reactor temperature from 340 to 360 °C need not. Why the difference?

Compare with the explanation

A bar encodes a value by its length, so the reader compares lengths; cutting the bottom off changes the ratio of lengths. A line or a point encodes a value by its position, so the reader compares positions against the axis; a narrow range shows small changes that matter, such as 5 °C in a reactor. The axis must still be labelled, and the range should match the changes that matter for the process, not be chosen to make a change look large.

In every month the mean is above the median. What does that say about benzene, and which would you report?

Compare with the explanation

The distribution is skewed to the right: most hours are low, and a few rush-hour hours are much higher and pull the mean up. The median describes a typical hour; the mean is what an exposure or an emission total depends on. Report the one that answers the question, say which one it is, and show the spread, for example the middle half of the hours.
