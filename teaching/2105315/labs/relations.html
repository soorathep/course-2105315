03

Predict

Remove the slow seasonal change from both series (a 29-day centered rolling mean) and correlate what is left: the day-to-day ups and downs.

Why 29 days: about a month, long enough that the weekly traffic cycle and single weather events stay out of the trend, and an odd number so the window centers on one day. A day gets a trend value only if at least 15 of its 29 daily means exist (more than half).

Check the AI's code

The analyst asked an AI assistant: "Does temperature affect NOx in this data?" It returned this.

daily = pd.read_csv("nox_temp_daily.csv", index_col="date")r = daily["T_degC"].corr(daily["NOx_ppb"])print(f"r = {r:.2f}, r² = {r**2:.0%}")# "Temperature explains about 30 % of NOx: colder days cause higher NOx."

The number is computed correctly. What is wrong with the sentence under it, and what would you do before writing any sentence?

Compare with the explanation

"Explains" and "cause" are claims the correlation cannot make. Between summer and autumn, temperature falls and NOx rises, and other things change with the season too, for example traffic returning after the summer holidays, heating, and shallower mixing of the air in autumn. Any two series that both drift through a season correlate, whether or not one acts on the other.

Before writing the sentence, remove what both series share (here the seasonal trend; in other data it may be the hour of day or the plant load) and look at whether the day-to-day changes still move together. Then say what you tested and what you did not.

trend = daily.rolling(29, center=True, min_periods=15).mean() print((daily - trend)["T_degC"].corr((daily - trend)["NOx_ppb"]))

08

Check your understanding

Write first, then compare.

After removing the trend your correlation was small. Does that prove temperature has no effect on NOx?

Compare with the explanation

No. It shows that, in this window, day-to-day temperature changes do not move NOx much; it says nothing certain about the seasonal part, where temperature and the other seasonal causes cannot be separated with this data. Showing that temperature acts on NOx needs a variable that changes temperature alone (an experiment, a cold spell in a stable traffic period) or a mechanism you can measure. Correlation can point to a question; it cannot settle it.

The 3σ rule waits for one day far from the mean. CUSUM adds up small differences. Why does CUSUM usually find a slow drift earlier?

Compare with the explanation

A drift of one standard deviation hardly ever produces a single day beyond 3σ, so the 3σ rule may wait weeks. CUSUM keeps a running total of the excess over the allowance; a small but persistent shift adds up day after day and crosses h within a few days. The last day the sum was zero is also a useful estimate of when the shift began, which the 3σ rule cannot give. The price is more false alarms if k and h are set too low.
