Activity 1 · The AI’s t-test

Pairs · 10 minutes · Lab 06, Step 4

before = df.loc[df.period == "before", "C6H6_ugm3"].dropna()after = df.loc[df.period == "after", "C6H6_ugm3"].dropna()t, p = stats.ttest_ind(after, before, equal_var=False)print(f"n = {len(before) + len(after)}, p = {p:.1e}")# "p < 0.05: the traffic plan changed benzene."

The code runs and p is computed correctly for what it was given. Which assumption of the t-test does this data break, and what does that do to p?

Studying alone: write your answer, then go on.

What to change

The assumption The t-test treats 667 hours as 667 independent measurements. Neighboring hours correlate at 0.84, and every day repeats its rush hours. The standard error is too small, so p is too small: for Demo A, 0.00006.

A first fix One value per day: 14 against 14. For Demo A, p becomes 0.13 and the 95 % confidence interval widens from 1.6 to 4.5 µg/m³. The comment’s “changed” also claims a cause that no test checked.

Count what is independent before you count values.

Placebo dates

Lab 06, Step 7: 306 dates, 14 days each side. Data: De Vito, UCI Air Quality, doi:10.24432/C59K5F, CC BY 4.0.

Why the tests fire too often

Hours are not independent Neighboring hours correlate at 0.84 and the daily cycle repeats. 667 hours carry far less information than 667 values.

Days are not either Neighboring days correlate at 0.6: weather lasts several days. 14 days count as fewer than 14.

Fortnights differ Season and holidays move the level. The t-test sees only the scatter inside each fortnight, not how much ordinary fortnights differ.

Placebo dates measure the third point directly: they are the year’s own record of “nothing changed”.

What you can claim

Demo A · 29 July 2004 −1.7 µg/m³ (95 % confidence interval −3.9 to 0.5), p = 0.13 on daily means. 45 % of ordinary dates show a difference this large. The data do not show an effect.

Demo B · 7 November 2004 −5.9 µg/m³ (−8.9 to −3.0), p = 0.0004. Only 1 of 306 ordinary dates shows a difference this large. A real shift, but the data cannot say whether the traffic plan or the November weather caused it.

Before a decision, ask for a comparison that rules out the other cause: a station the plan did not touch, or the same weeks in another year.
