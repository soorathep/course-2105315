Two ways to measure spread

Seven hours of benzene, µg/m³. One of them is a fault: 4.1 · 4.4 · 4.0 · 4.6 · 4.3 · 21.0 · 4.2

Mean and standard deviation Mean 6.66, σ 6.33 (pandas .std()). The one bad hour pulls both up.

The 21.0 is (21.0 − 6.66) / 6.33 = 2.3 σ from the mean. Below 3: not flagged.

Median and MAD Median 4.3, the middle value once sorted. MAD is the median of the distances from the median: 0.2, 0.1, 0.3, 0.3, 0, 16.7, 0.1, so MAD = 0.2.

Scaled MAD = 1.4826 × 0.2 = 0.30 µg/m³. The factor makes it equal to σ for normally distributed data. The 21.0 is (21.0 − 4.3) / 0.30 ≈ 56 scaled MADs away. Flagged.

One bad value can hide by inflating σ. The median and the MAD barely move, which is why the Hampel filter on the next slide uses them.

Three ways to call a value unusual

Far from the whole record z-score. Distance from the mean, in standard deviations (σ). Flag if more than 3σ. Ignores time: a normal rush hour can be “far”.

Far from its neighbors Hampel filter. Distance from the median of the 7 nearby hours, in scaled MADs (1.4826 × MAD, equal to σ for normal data). Flag if more than 3. Follows the daily cycle; also flags sharp real changes.

Not seen by a second measurement Physical check. Does another instrument, a mass balance or a lab sample agree? The only one that asks whether something happened.

The first two ask “is it unusual?”. Only the third asks “is it real?”. A real event can be unusual; a fault can look ordinary.

Why a z-score fails on a daily cycle

What the z-score sees One number for the whole two weeks: mean 10.5, standard deviation 7.2. Anything above 32.1 is “unusual”. A busy morning reaches that. A drop to 0.1 at noon is well inside the range.

What it misses At noon benzene is normally 10 to 20. A value of 0.1 is unusual for that hour, not for the record. The faults also inflate the standard deviation, which makes the rule even less sensitive.

In Demo A the z-score flags 4 hours: 2 faults and 2 real rush hours. It misses 6 of the 8 faults.

Use a rule that knows the time of day (Hampel), and check flagged hours against a second measurement before you remove anything.
