One correlation, three views

Lab 05, Demo A, 20 June to 9 October 2004. Data: De Vito, UCI Air Quality, doi:10.24432/C59K5F, CC BY 4.0.

What r measures

What it is How closely the points follow one straight line, from −1 to +1. The sign gives the direction.

What it is not Not the slope, not the size of an effect, and not a direction of cause. r = −0.57 does not say temperature changes NOx.

What feeds it Any variation the two series share. A slow trend that both follow, over weeks, can produce most of r.

r² = 0.32 is often read as “temperature explains 32 % of NOx”. That sentence claims a cause the number cannot carry.

Activity 1 · Correlated or driven?

Pairs · 10 minutes · no computer needed

Cooling-water return temperature and product impurity rise together on most afternoons.

Third variable: the time of day. Ambient heat raises both. Compare afternoons with afternoons.

Over a year, steam use and reactor yield fall together.

Third variable: throughput. Low-load months use less steam and run off the design point.

Days with more operator log entries have more off-spec batches.

Reverse direction: problems cause the log entries, not the other way round.

After a new catalyst lot arrived, conversion rose. Feed purity also improved that month.

Two changes at once. The data cannot separate them. Session 11 returns to this.

Column pressure and top temperature move together within every hour.

Driven, with a mechanism: boiling point rises with pressure. The physics gives the direction.

Daily temperature and NOx: r = −0.57 over 16 summer weeks.

Mostly the calendar: holidays and season move both. After removing the trend, r = 0.14.

For each pair: does one drive the other, does something else move both, or can you not tell? Say what you would check.

Checks before a cause

Remove what both share Subtract each series’ slow trend, or compare like with like: same hour, same load, same season.

Look at the timing A cause comes first. Does one series change before the other, by a consistent lag?

Name a mechanism Write the physical or chemical reason. If you cannot, say “associated with”, not “causes”.

Change it on purpose A planned step test or trial settles what observation cannot. Weeks 6 and 7.

A small r after removing the trend does not prove “no effect” either. It says the day-to-day data show little. Lab 05, Step 8 asks you to explain this.
