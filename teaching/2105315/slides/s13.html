Two files, two clocks

Lab 07, Demo A: sensor PT08.S1(CO) and benzene C6H6(GT), 13 to 15 September 2004. Data: De Vito, UCI Air Quality, doi:10.24432/C59K5F, CC BY 4.0.

Activity 1 · What would you check first?

Pairs · 10 minutes · no computer needed

The reactor logger exports in UTC. The lab LIMS stamps samples in local time.

The clock. Convert one to the other, then confirm with a lag scan on a variable both see.

The lab assay is taken once a day at about 09:00. The sensor logs every minute.

Join the slow to the fast: the sensor value nearest the sample time (pandas merge_asof), or its mean over the sampling window.

The export contains −200 in some cells and blanks in others.

Missing codes: both mean "no value". Convert before any mean (Lab 01).

After a network outage the logger re-sent its buffer. Some rows appear twice.

Repeated rows: count them and drop exact duplicates before a merge copies them.

On 31 October the 02:00 hour appears twice; on 28 March there is no 02:00.

Daylight saving. Store in UTC, convert only for display.

A flow meter reads exactly 41.2 for nine hours.

A stuck value, not a steady plant. Treat as missing after checking the log (Lab 03).

For each file: what is the problem, what would it do to the result if missed, and which check finds it?

The path to a decision

Step The question Week
Audit What is in each file: rows, codes for missing, repeated rows, the clock? 1
Clean Which values are missing, and which are impossible? Fill only what you can defend. 2, 3
Align Do the two files mean the same hour? Check with a lag scan. 5, 7
Analyze Calibrate on one period, test on another. Compare with what ordinary periods give. 5, 6
Decide State the decision, the numbers, what you did to the data, and the limits. 4, 7

An error in an early step does not show as an error later. It shows as a confident wrong number.
