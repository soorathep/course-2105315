What p measures

Lab 06, Demo A: benzene, 15 July to 11 August 2004, change on 29 July. Data: De Vito, UCI Air Quality, doi:10.24432/C59K5F, CC BY 4.0.

Reading p

What it is If nothing had changed, the share of comparisons that would show a difference at least this large. Demo A: about 13 %.

What it is not Not the probability that the change worked, and not the size of the change. p = 0.001 can belong to a difference too small to matter.

What it assumes That the values are independent and that “nothing changed” is the only alternative. Session 12 tests both on plant data.

0.05 is a convention, not a law. Report the p you got, not only which side of 0.05 it fell on.

Activity 1 · Significant, or important?

Pairs · 10 minutes · no computer needed

Yield rose from 91.2 to 91.3 %, p = 0.0004, over 40 000 batches.

Significant, not important: 0.1 point is real but may not pay for the change. Ask for the size in money.

Energy use fell by 8 %, p = 0.20, over six weeks.

Not shown, not disproved. The interval is wide: it includes zero and a large saving. Collect more weeks.

"p = 0.03, so there is a 97 % chance the new catalyst works."

Wrong reading. p is computed assuming no change. It is not the probability of either hypothesis.

Twenty tags were compared before and after a shutdown. One gave p = 0.04.

Expected by chance: one in twenty tests falls below 0.05 when nothing changed.

Off-spec rate before 4.1 %, after 2.9 %; 95 % interval of the change −2.0 to −0.4 points.

Useful report: size and interval. The interval excludes zero, so p < 0.05 without saying it.

Benzene: 1.7 µg/m³ lower after the change, p = 0.13 on daily means.

Not shown. Shuffled labels give a difference this large about one time in eight.

For each result: is the change shown, is it large enough to matter, and is the sentence a correct reading of p?

Size, interval, p

Report this The difference with its unit, a 95 % interval, the number of independent values, and p. Demo A: −1.7 µg/m³ (−3.9 to 0.5), 14 + 14 days, p = 0.13.

Then say What the interval allows: “a fall of up to 3.9 or a rise of up to 0.5 µg/m³ fits these data”. And what else changed in the same weeks.

The interval carries more than p: its width shows how much the data can tell, and its position shows the size.
