What to change

Two clocks, one name Renaming both columns to “t” hides that one is UTC. The merge pairs each sensor hour with benzene two hours later. Add two hours to the analyzer stamps, and confirm with a lag scan: r 0.54 becomes 0.93 for Demo A.

Repeated rows Three sensor rows appear twice, and the merge copies their analyzer rows. Drop exact duplicates first. The effect is small here; the audit takes one line.

Neither problem raises an error. Both change the number that the decision rests on.

The decision memo

1 · The decision One sentence the manager can act on. Demo A: the sensor may run the alert.

2 · The evidence Numbers with their basis: 146 of 161 hours above 15 µg/m³ flagged (91 %), 35 false alarms.

3 · What you did Removed 3 repeated rows; added 2 h to the UTC stamps (r 0.54 to 0.93); calibrated on 5 to 11 Sep.

4 · The limits Three autumn weeks only. The sensor drifts (Lab 05): check it against the analyzer each month.

A reviewer with your two files and your memo should get the same numbers. That is what makes the decision defensible.

What the course covered

Week The question What you can now do
1 Can I trust this file? Audit rows, codes for missing, units, the clock
2 What do I do with gaps? Choose a fill you can defend, or leave the gap
3 Spike or real event? Flag with robust rules; smooth without moving peaks
4 Does the figure tell the truth? Choose the view; start bars at zero; state the window
5 Correlated, or driven? Remove shared trends; find a drift with CUSUM
6 Did the change work? Count independent values; compare with ordinary periods
7 What do I decide? Run every check in order and write a memo that can be checked

In every week the AI wrote the code. Your part was to know what to ask, to read what came back, and to check the numbers.
